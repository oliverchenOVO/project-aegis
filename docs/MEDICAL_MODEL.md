# Cohort medical model

Systemic earthquake content 2.1.0 and flood content 1.1.0 enable this model through explicit hospital clinical capacities. Released earthquake v1 content and the frozen v0.1 engine retain their original medical flow. New fields are optional without schema defaults, preserving legacy hashes.

Each patient record represents a counted injury cohort and owns one civilian cohort. Splitting a patient for transport or partial admission splits the matching civilian population, preserving its origin source exactly. An ambulance carries at most two people, rather than two arbitrarily large records. Splits and transport are deterministic, and queues contain unique patient IDs.

Triage has MINOR, DELAYED, IMMEDIATE and EXPECTANT categories. Severity and current health determine escalation; a cohort can deteriorate from delayed care into critical/expectant status. Waiting patients receive limited field stabilization from an operating EMS unit. Transit, handover and queue time continue deterioration. Overdue transport emits a factual delay, followed by directly referenced deterioration/death events. These are game simulation rates, not clinical recommendations.

Arrival starts a real ambulance HANDOVER state. A base 20 seconds, carried severity, emergency queue length and hospital emergency status determine the bounded transfer duration (maximum 240 seconds). The vehicle cannot accept reassignment or recall during transport/handover. After completion, cohorts enter the hospital queue and the vehicle resumes its actual route/mission. Start, completion and queued events reference the transported patient and infrastructure causes, including when every carried patient dies before completion.

Hospitals distinguish total beds, ED queue capacity, ICU and trauma beds, staffing and treatment throughput. A deterministic fractional credit accumulates per second, capped at four people; admissions consume credit, free inpatient beds and the relevant specialty capacity. IMMEDIATE patients have priority, then DELAYED, MINOR and EXPECTANT, with health and stable ID tie breakers. Partial admission creates a conserved patient/cohort split. ED capacity represents congestion rather than silently discarding arrivals: overcrowding triggers DIVERTING status and worsens queue deterioration.

NORMAL, STRAINED, OVER_CAPACITY, DIVERTING and POWER_EMERGENCY derive from actual census, queue and powered state. Status events, overload ticks and power emergency ticks are authoritative replay state. Destination selection penalizes unavailable power, load and diversion, while still requiring an actual passable EMS route. Backup fuel, generator failure and baseline critical deaths remain shared infrastructure effects.

Powered treatment improves health according to staffing. Unpowered treatment deteriorates health and reduces admissions throughput to 20%. Full recovery emits a counted PATIENT_RECOVERED event, frees the inpatient bed and records outcome/treatment completion. Death removes the cohort from queues/vehicles and emits a counted outcome with direct medical/infrastructure references. After each tick, census includes handover, queue and admitted patients and excludes recovered/dead cohorts.

Invariants check source population conservation, patient/cohort counts, unique queues, matching hospital references, positive counts, bounded health, two-person ambulance capacity, handover location/deadline and inpatient bed capacity. Checkpoints and saves contain queue order, credit, counters, triage, vehicle deadlines and every RNG stream. No wall clock drives this flow.

Validation: eight medical tests cover priority/ICU/throughput, ICU escalation when specialty beds are full, congested vehicle handover, outage queue deterioration/death causation, real exposure cohort splitting, explicit recovery and two full dispatch/replay/checkpoint/save runs. Three full flood seed performance runs are recorded in `qa/medical-performance.json`; these are milestone checks, not the final 200-seed release gate.

One focused flood regression run exceeded its unchanged 30-second per-test limit while multiple validation workloads competed for resources. Redundant checkpoint hashing, an unnecessary pre-validation record clone and repeated manifest decoding/parsing were removed without changing hash algorithms or integrity checks. The following complete 57-test run passed, including the ownership and forged-checkpoint tests. The ICU escalation test subsequently adds the 58th check. The final 200-seed benchmark remains pending.
